# Methodology (G3 decided 2026-11-15; see D7 in decisions.md)

Parameters live in `models/*.yaml` and `models/salt_tolerance.csv`, each with `evidence` and `confidence`. The result tables below are generated by `scripts/build_methodology.py` from the engines and the downloaded NASA POWER files.

Two terms: an **input check** compares our inputs with ground data (it shows the inputs are sound, not that outputs are right); a **plausibility check** asks whether outputs agree with published findings and move the right way when an input changes. We cannot show that following the tool improves results on a real farm, and we will not say so.

## What was chosen, and by whom
Tarek asked on 2026-11-15 for two independent reviews of the method options and for the choice to follow from them. Two read-only AI reviewers (science and code; strategy and judging) recommended the options below. The choice is logged as D7; Tarek can overrule it.

| Engine | Chosen | Why | Not chosen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wheat calendar | **B, thermal time**, shown only as a comparison of sowing dates | Fixes A's unrealistic fixed season length; runs on data we hold | A (unrealistic for late sowings); C (a 250 m pixel mixes plots; MODIS used only as a check on the calendar) |
| Water | **A, FAO-56 crop water need**, as ratios between crops plus a plain-arithmetic canal-turn check | Transparent; NASA POWER is central | B (needs soil and application assumptions we cannot check); C (only if Hussain's MOD16 data arrives) |
| Salt | **A, tolerance lookup** from a soil test or three sign classes; the first thing cut if time runs short | Small effort; honest that no NASA salinity product exists | B (1 km soil units do not describe a field); C (needs ground samples) |

## Changes made after the reviews
- **Calendar engine:** one thermal-time constant for the whole Delta, calibrated once at Gharbia (before, every site was calibrated to mature on the same date, so the maturity date was true by construction); a missing day no longer crashes the engine and dropped seasons are counted and reported; percentiles fixed (they were uneven); heat compared at 30, 32 and 34 C; results stated relative to a 15 November sowing, not as absolute heat days.
- **Outputs:** rice water need is labelled a floor, since FAO-56 leaves out ponding and percolation; millimetres are marked unchecked; Damietta carries a sea-cell caution; Menoufia and Sharqia are one weather cell.
- **Rotation module** (`src/fieldshift/rotation.py`): joins the three engines into a comparison of three rotations (the farmer's existing one plus two alternatives) with evidence, gaps and risks for each, because the brief asks for a rotation comparison.

## Engine 1: wheat calendar
Question: if wheat is sown on date X, how does the heat met during grain filling compare with other dates, over 35 seasons (1990-2025)?
Method: degree-days from daily (Tmax+Tmin)/2 with base 0 C accumulate from sowing to one maturity target (calibrated so a 15 November sowing matures about 25 April at Gharbia); grain filling is the last 30% of thermal time; a hot day is Tmax at or above 32 C.

### Wheat heat by sowing date (32 C, method B; median hot days in grain filling, 10th-90th percentile; extra days vs 15 Nov at 30/32/34 C)

| Sown | Gharbia | Kafr El-Sheikh | Beheira | Menoufia / Sharqia (same cell) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01/11 | 4 (1-8); -6/-3/-3 | 3 (0-8); -4/-4/-3 | 1 (0-4); -2/-2/-1 | 5 (2-10); -8/-4/-3 |
| 15/11 | 7 (4-13); +0/+0/+0 | 7 (3-11); +0/+0/+0 | 3 (1-6); +0/+0/+0 | 9 (5-17); +0/+0/+0 |
| 01/12 | 13 (9-16); +6/+6/+4 | 12 (8-16); +7/+5/+4 | 5 (3-10); +4/+2/+2 | 15 (12-19); +5/+6/+5 |
| 15/12 | 17 (12-22); +11/+10/+7 | 14 (11-21); +10/+7/+5 | 7 (4-12); +6/+4/+2 | 19 (16-24); +10/+10/+8 |
| 10/01 | 23 (18-30); +16/+16/+10 | 21 (16-27); +15/+14/+10 | 11 (7-15); +13/+8/+5 | 25 (21-30); +14/+16/+13 |

Median maturity for a 15 Nov sowing (one thermal constant for all sites): gharbia 25 Apr, kafr_el_sheikh 26 Apr, beheira 27 Apr, menoufia 26 Apr

Reference thermal-time constant: 2758 degree-days (base 0.0 C) from sowing to maturity.

**Plausibility check (direction only):** heat exposure rises steadily with later sowing at every site and every threshold; a 10 January sowing meets about three times the hot days of a 15 November sowing at Gharbia. This agrees in direction with elbasyoni2018 (full text), where wheat sown 7-13 January lost about 40% of yield against mid-November sowing, partly through more rust. We do not convert hot days into yield loss: there is no source for that.

**Limits**
- The 32 C threshold, the 30% grain-filling share, the base temperature and the late-April maturity target have no full-text source (`confidence: low`). The mid-November recommended date and the late-sowing contrast are from elbasyoni2018. A reviewer's search snippet (not read in full) puts the wheat anthesis limit near 31 C (Porter and Gawith 1999); that is an anthesis figure, not a grain-filling one.
- Thermal time only: no vernalisation or photoperiod, which probably makes late-sown wheat mature later than modelled. Anthesis, the most heat-sensitive stage, is not counted separately.
- Reanalysis temperature at about 50 km, **not yet checked against Delta weather stations.** A reviewer's search snippet says POWER overestimated Tmax by under 3 C at about 80% of Egyptian stations (not verified). Suggested check: NOAA GSOD or ISD stations (Tanta, Mansoura, Baltim, Port Said), March-May Tmax 2010-2024; if the bias is large, shift the threshold by the bias.
- **Damietta's cell is mostly sea**: zero hot days at every date. Do not show it as safe. Menoufia and Sharqia share one cell.
- Only heat is counted; rust, water and salt are not in this engine.

## Engine 2: water
Question: how much water do the summer crops need here, and what must each canal turn supply?
Method: FAO-56 Penman-Monteith reference evapotranspiration (ET0) computed daily from POWER temperature, humidity, wind and solar radiation; crop evapotranspiration is Kc x ET0 summed over the FAO-56 stage lengths. Kc and stage lengths are from FAO-56 Tables 11-12 as shown on the FAO web page (digits to be re-checked in the PDF). Four building blocks (saturation vapour pressure, its slope, pressure at 1800 m, extraterrestrial radiation) match FAO-56 worked values in the tests.
Canal-turn check: peak daily need x days between turns. Plain arithmetic, no soil-storage assumption.
Data finding: IMERG over the whole Delta box gives 14-181 mm of rain a year (2025 is January-September), almost none from May to September, so summer crops depend entirely on irrigation.

### Summer crop water need (FAO-56 ETc, mm per season, median and 10th-90th percentile over 1990-2024)

| Crop | Gharbia | Kafr El-Sheikh | Beheira | Menoufia / Sharqia |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rice (15/05, 150 d) | 1203 (1172-1236) | 1189 (1159-1220) | 1105 (1066-1138) | 1284 (1257-1321) |
| maize (01/05, 150 d) | 916 (894-940) | 905 (887-928) | 840 (815-863) | 977 (955-1000) |
| cotton (01/04, 195 d) | 1201 (1171-1229) | 1187 (1160-1214) | 1099 (1067-1134) | 1283 (1256-1313) |

Gharbia annual reference ET0 (mean of full years): 1857 mm.

**Limits**
- **ET0 looks high** (about 1,857 mm a year at Gharbia, 8 mm/day in June). I have no source read in full for a typical Delta value, so I cannot say it is wrong; it is probably the warm POWER bias above. **Use ratios between crops, not millimetres, until checked.**
- Rice needs more than the FAO figure: ponding, puddling, percolation and seepage are excluded, so rice is a floor and comparisons with rice understate the other crops' relative advantage.
- No canal supply, rain, capillary rise or irrigation losses. Sowing dates are assumptions unless the farmer states them.
- Hussain's MOD16 evapotranspiration request would be an input check if it arrives.

## Engine 3: salt
Question: which crops can the farmer's soil take?
Method: FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 29 Rev.1, Table 4 (ECe at 100, 90, 75 and 50% yield), read from the FAO web page and linearly interpolated; fine at 90% or above, reduced at 75-90%, not advised below 75%. Input is a soil test, or one of three sign classes (none, patches, white crust) mapped to assumed ECe of 2, 6 and 12 dS/m. **That mapping is the team's assumption, not a measurement.**

### Salt: yield potential (%) by soil salinity, FAO paper 29 table linearly interpolated

| Crop | ECe 2 | ECe 4 | ECe 6 | ECe 8 | ECe 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| wheat | 100 | 100 | 100 | 86 | 57 |
| rice | 100 | 88 | 64 | n/a (<50) | n/a (<50) |
| maize | 96 | 73 | n/a (<50) | n/a (<50) | n/a (<50) |
| cotton | 100 | 100 | 100 | 98 | 79 |
| berseem | 97 | 86 | 74 | 62 | n/a (<50) |

No NASA product measures soil salinity (NASA's salinity missions measure the ocean). The FAO table is a guide to relative, steady-state tolerance. Shallow saline groundwater can re-salinise a leached field (hagage2024). The rice trade-off (rice leaches salt but is the heaviest water user) is a caution from ghazouani2014 (full text) and kotb2000 (abstract only), not a calculation.

## How the engines meet
`rotation.py`: the farmer's existing rotation plus alternatives, each one summer crop then wheat. For each: summer water need as a ratio to the existing one, heat for the wheat at its sowing date, salt fit of both crops, and why it was included, what supports it, the gaps and the risks. It does not pick a single winner. Not modelled: nitrogen fixation and soil organic matter, pests and disease, prices and income, labour and machinery, rice ponding and percolation, the farmer's actual canal supply.
